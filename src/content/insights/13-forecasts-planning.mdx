A single confident number invites an argument you can't win. A range you can defend — with the assumptions on the table — turns a forecast into a planning tool instead of a promise you'll be held to. Disclosure standards now expect exactly this: forward-looking estimates shown with their basis, not a lone point estimate. The honesty is what makes it useful. An investor trusts the team that shows its working far more than the one that quietly hopes the number lands.

Where this fits: [clear reporting](/services/) — forecasts and packs that hold up under questions.
