title: "The board doesn't want a dashboard. It wants a decision."
takeaway: "Reporting earns its place when it ends an argument in the room — not when it adds one more screen no one opens between meetings."
order: 1
---

A report that doesn't change what happens in the room is overhead. The test is simple: did it settle a question, or just add another tab nobody opens? Build the pack backwards from the decision it has to support — the approval, the go/no-go, the number the board will be asked about — and most of the clutter falls away on its own. What's left is the handful of figures that actually move the conversation.
