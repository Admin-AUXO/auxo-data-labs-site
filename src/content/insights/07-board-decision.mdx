title: "The board doesn't want a dashboard. It wants a decision."
takeaway: "Reporting earns its place when it settles a question in the room, not when it adds one more screen no one opens between meetings."
order: 7
---

A board or investor update isn't there to show charts. It's there to decide something: approve this hire, cut that spend, defend a number next week. So build the report backwards from the decision it serves, and most of the clutter goes. The test for any chart is simple: did it settle a question, or open another tab nobody clicks? Keep the few figures that move the decision. Everything else is decoration you pay to maintain.

Where this fits: [Build](/how-it-works/). Reporting that holds up when the board, investors or auditors ask hard questions.
