---
topic: "Forecasting"
title: "Forecasts are for planning, not for promising."
takeaway: "A range you can defend to an investor beats a single confident number you can't. Show the assumptions, and the conversation changes."
order: 17
---

A single confident number invites an argument you can't win. A range you can defend — with the assumptions on the table — turns a forecast into a planning tool instead of a promise you'll be held to. The honesty is what makes it useful: an investor trusts the team that shows its working far more than the one that quietly hopes the point estimate lands.

Where this fits: [clear reporting](/services/) — forecasts and packs that hold up under questions.
