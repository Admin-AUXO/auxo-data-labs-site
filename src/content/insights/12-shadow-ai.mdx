The risk isn't usually the AI you approved. It's the AI quietly stitched into a spreadsheet, a plugin, or a vendor tool no one logged. You can't govern what you haven't listed — so the first step isn't a policy, it's an inventory: what's running, on whose data, deciding what. Once it's written down, the easy calls (turn this off, watch that one) become obvious. Visibility comes before control.

Where this fits: [reliable automation](/services/) — knowing what runs, on what data, with a person accountable.
