The AI that should worry you usually isn't the one you approved. It's the model stitched into a spreadsheet, the plugin someone switched on, the vendor feature nobody logged — quietly making decisions on your data while leadership assumes it doesn't exist. You can't govern what you haven't listed, which is exactly why NIST's framework opens with Map: know what's running, on whose data, deciding what, before you reach for a policy. The inventory is dull and it's the cheapest control you'll ever build. Visibility first. Guardrails second.

Where this fits: [reliable automation](/services/) — knowing what runs, on what data, with a person accountable.
