title: "The board doesn't want a dashboard. It wants a decision."
takeaway: "Reporting earns its place when it ends an argument in the room — not when it adds one more screen no one opens between meetings."
order: 1
---

A dashboard that doesn't change what happens in the room is overhead. The test for any report is simple: did it settle a question, or just add another tab nobody opens between meetings? Build reporting backwards from the decision it has to support, and most of the clutter falls away on its own.
