Regulatory reach increasingly follows the customer, not the company. The EU AI Act, like GDPR before it, applies extraterritorially: a Gulf PropTech firm or family office can fall in scope if it places an AI system on the EU market — or simply if the system's output is used there. High-risk providers outside the EU may need to appoint an authorised representative inside it. For anyone with European investors, tenants, or partners, "we're not an EU company" is no longer the end of the question. Map where your outputs land before you scale.

Where this fits: [reliable automation](/services/) — AI deployed with the cross-border rules in view, not after.
