**In short:** Most startups carry several versions of the same number. Founders know which one to quote in which room. An AI assistant doesn't, so write down what your key terms mean before you point it at your data.

## How many active customers do you have?

Say an investor asks the question on a call, and your sales lead, finance lead and head of product each reach for a different tab. You will probably get three answers.

| Team | Counts as "active" |
|---|---|
| Sales | Anyone with an open contract |
| Finance | Anyone billed this quarter |
| Product | Anyone who logged in last month |

Each is right by its own rule. The same happens with ARR, churn and runway. The deck, the model and the accounts disagree, and diligence is where investors notice.

<Callout>
An AI assistant picks a definition too, and you may never see which.
</Callout>

## Small mismatches get expensive

NASA lost the Mars Climate Orbiter in 1999 because its ground software used English units while the spacecraft's software used metric. The mismatch threw off the trajectory and sent the spacecraft too close to Mars ([NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-climate-orbiter/)).

Business mismatches are quieter. Gartner research from 2020 put the cost of poor data quality at an average of at least $12.9 million a year per organisation ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/data-analytics/topics/data-quality)).

## What does an AI assistant do with this?

Ask it for churn and it answers with confidence, built on whichever table it found first.

That is a set-up gap, not a flaw in the tool. Gartner found that 63% of organisations either don't have, or aren't sure they have, the right data management practices for AI. It predicts that through 2026, organisations will abandon 60% of AI projects that are not supported by AI-ready data ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-02-26-lack-of-ai-ready-data-puts-ai-projects-at-risk)).

## Start with your board deck

Take the terms in your board deck and data room: ARR, churn, burn, runway, active customer, pipeline. For each one, write down five things.

Then check each definition against the data. Does your CRM hold the field, filled in for most records?

Put the glossary where your tools read it, not in a slide deck. If you want a hand getting the investor-facing numbers to agree, AUXO's [Investor metrics](/marketplace/) service covers it.

## What to do next

Agree what the important words mean, where each number comes from and who owns it. It is the cheapest part of any AI project, and the easiest to skip.
