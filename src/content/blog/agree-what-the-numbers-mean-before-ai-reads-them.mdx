**In short:** Most startups carry several versions of the same number. Founders know which one to quote in which room. An AI assistant doesn't, so write down what your key terms mean before you point it at your data.

## One question, three answers

Ask sales, finance and product how many active customers you have. Sales counts open contracts. Finance counts anyone billed this quarter. Product counts anyone who logged in last month. Each is right by its own rule.

The same happens with ARR, churn and runway. The deck, the model and the accounts disagree, and diligence is where investors notice.

## Small mismatches, large costs

NASA lost the Mars Climate Orbiter in 1999 because its ground software used English units while the spacecraft's software used metric. The mismatch threw off the trajectory and sent the spacecraft too close to Mars ([NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-climate-orbiter/)). 

Business mismatches are quieter. Gartner research from 2020 put the cost of poor data quality at an average of at least $12.9 million a year per organisation ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/data-analytics/topics/data-quality)).

## AI picks a definition and doesn't say which

An AI assistant picks a definition too, and you may never see which. Ask it for churn and it answers with confidence, built on whichever table it found first.

Gartner found that 63% of organisations either don't have, or aren't sure they have, the right data management practices for AI. It predicts that through 2026, organisations will abandon 60% of AI projects that are not supported by AI-ready data ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-02-26-lack-of-ai-ready-data-puts-ai-projects-at-risk)).

## Start with the terms in your board deck

Take the terms in your board deck and data room: ARR, churn, burn, runway, active customer, pipeline. For each, write down:

- **Name:** for example, "active customer".
- **Definition:** one sentence, including what is left out.
- **Source:** the system and the field it comes from.
- **Owner:** the person who decides if it changes.
- **Last changed:** the date and the reason.

Check each definition against the data: does your CRM hold the field, filled in for most records? Then put the glossary where your tools read it, not in a slide deck. If you want a hand getting the investor-facing numbers to agree, AUXO's [Investor metrics](/marketplace/) service covers it.
