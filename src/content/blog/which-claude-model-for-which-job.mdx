**In short.** Anthropic offers four Claude models. They differ in depth, speed and cost, not in whether they are smart enough. Match the model to how often a task runs and what a wrong answer costs, then test on your own examples.

## The four current models

Each one is good. Pick by the job.

- **Claude Fable 5.2.** Anthropic's most capable model open to all customers. Anthropic positions it for demanding reasoning and long-running agent work: sessions that run for hours, multistep research, and analysis carried through to a finished document, spreadsheet or deck. It is the slowest and the most expensive of the four.
- **Claude Opus 5.5.** Built for long-running coding and knowledge work. Anthropic's own guidance is to start with Opus 5.5 for most workloads. It is moderate on speed and sits between Fable and Sonnet on cost.
- **Claude Sonnet 5.5.** Described as the best combination of speed and intelligence. It suits everyday work: data analysis, content creation, reading images and documents, and tool use. It is fast and costs less than Opus.
- **Claude Haiku 5.5.** For high-volume, time-sensitive tasks such as classification, extraction and routing. It is the fastest and by far the cheapest.

All four read text and images, work across languages and can use tools. All four share the same very large context window and the same knowledge cutoff. So the choice is not about features. It is about how much thinking a task needs, how fast you need the answer, and how many times you will run it.

## Match the model to the work

Treat this as a starting point, not a rule.

**Finance.** A draft of the investor update, with commentary on why ARR and churn moved, needs judgement and care with numbers. Opus 5.5 is a sensible start. Tagging thousands of expense lines to the right category is repetitive and well defined. That is Haiku territory.

**Sales.** Sorting inbound leads by segment and urgency suits Haiku. Drafting an account brief from your CRM notes and public sources suits Sonnet or Opus.

**Support.** Tagging tickets by product and urgency suits Haiku. Drafting a reply to a frustrated customer who has written three times suits Sonnet.

**Engineering.** Summarising a pull request or sorting bug reports suits Sonnet or Haiku. Planning a refactor across several services is long-running work for Opus or Fable.

**Content.** First drafts of posts and summaries are Sonnet's everyday work. A long market report that has to hold together across many sources is where Opus or Fable earns the extra cost.

## Two ways to start

Anthropic describes two approaches.

**Efficiency first.** Start with Haiku, test it on your real task, and move up only where it falls short. This suits prototypes, high-volume work and tight budgets.

**Capability first.** Start with Opus 5.5 and get the task working well. Then try lowering the effort setting or moving to a smaller model. If Opus at its highest settings still falls short on very demanding work, try Fable.

Both end in the same place. You test on your own work, then decide. Anthropic says a good evaluation set is the most important step.

## The model is rarely the hardest decision

The same model gives very different results depending on what it reads, what it is allowed to do and who checks its work.

A poor answer about last quarter's margin is rarely a model problem. It is usually two systems that define "revenue" differently. A bigger model will not fix that. It will give you a more articulate wrong answer.

## Try this

Pick one recurring task. Write down three things:

1. How often it runs: daily, weekly, or thousands of times a month.
2. What a wrong answer would cost: a small fix, an awkward email, or a bad decision in front of your board.
3. Who checks the output today.

High volume and low cost of error points to Haiku. Low volume and high cost of error points to Opus or Fable. Everything between is a good place to try Sonnet. Run ten real examples through your choice and through the model one step up, and compare the results side by side.
