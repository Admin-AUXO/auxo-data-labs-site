**In short:** Reports are easy to add and hard to retire, so the pile grows until nobody trusts it. Give each recurring report one owner, one decision it serves and a review date. Archive the rest.

## How the pile grows

Someone asks a question. A report answers it and keeps running. Adding a report takes one request. Retiring one takes agreement from everyone who might still look at it. So the pile only grows. We call the result operating debt: the running cost of reporting that no longer serves a decision.

In a startup it looks like three versions of "monthly revenue" across the CRM, the billing tool and the board deck, and someone reconciling them by hand the night before the meeting.

In a survey of 566 data and analytics leaders, fewer than half (44%) said their team was effective at providing value to the organisation. In the same survey, 44% said their teams had grown in the past year ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/03-21-2023-gartner-survey-reveals-less-than-half-of-data-and-analytics-teams-effectively-provide-value-to-the-organization)).

The signs: reports that outlive their decisions, half of them still on last year's revenue rule, only the builder able to explain them, and alarm when anyone suggests switching one off.

## AI reads the stale report too

A person learns which report to ignore. An AI assistant doesn't. Connect one to your shared drive or warehouse and it reads the stale report alongside the live one. Microsoft makes the same point to its own customers. It says Copilot and agents work best when content is current and well governed, and it recommends archiving inactive sites so answers draw on current material ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot/get-ready-copilot-sharepoint-advanced-management)).

Operating debt used to cost hours. Now it can produce confident wrong answers. A diligence team that finds two versions of the same metric in your data room will ask which is true.

## Three things every report needs

1. **An owner.** One named person, not a team.
2. **A decision it serves.** One sentence: who uses it, for which call.
3. **A review date.** Every 90 days is a sensible start.

At each review, the owner confirms the decision it still serves, or the report is archived with a note on why and where its replacement lives. Archive rather than delete.

The first pass is a spreadsheet with one row per report and an afternoon of short conversations.

## When not to bother

With ten reports, you need one hour and a list. The rule earns its keep when nobody can say how many exist. Don't retire a report just because it is old: a cash report you read every Friday is doing its job.
