**In short:** an AI policy for a startup fits on one page. It names the approved tools, the data that never goes in, who approves what leaves the company and who to tell when something goes wrong. Copy the template below and finish it in a week.

Your team already uses AI. Some people use a work account. Some use a personal one, with customer data in it.

Then an investor or customer asks what your AI policy says. If nothing is written down, nobody knows what is allowed.

## Keep the rule to one page

A long policy gets signed and never read. A one-page policy gets used. A policy that bans everything gets ignored, and people use their phones instead. One that allows what is safe gives them an approved way to work.

Who owns each tool is a separate question, answered in [AI governance for a 50-person company](/blog/ai-governance-for-a-50-person-company/).

## Five questions the policy must answer

1. **What data may go into an AI tool?** Name what never goes in, and what is fine.
2. **Which tools are approved?** List them by name and say which account type. Vendors treat plan types differently. OpenAI, for example, [says it does not train on business data by default](https://openai.com/enterprise-privacy/), while consumer plans work differently. Read each tool's data page.
3. **Who approves AI output before it leaves?** A person approves anything sent to a customer, an investor or a supplier.
4. **Who owns the data and the work?** Both stay with the company, subject to your contracts and local law.
5. **What happens when something goes wrong?** Who to tell, and how fast.

## A template to copy

Paste this into a document and replace every bracket.

> **[Company] AI policy**
>
> **1. Purpose.** This policy sets the rules for using AI tools at [Company]. It applies to every employee, contractor and intern.
>
> **2. Approved tools.** Use only these tools, on company accounts: [Tool A], [Tool B], [Tool C]. [Name] keeps this list. To request a new tool, ask [Name].
>
> **3. Data that never goes into an AI tool.** Customer personal data. Information covered by an NDA. Investor-confidential material. Payroll and HR records. Passwords, keys and access tokens. Unreleased financial results. [Add your own.]
>
> **4. Data that may go in.** Public information. Your own drafts. Internal material marked [Level], in approved tools only.
>
> **5. Approval before release.** AI may draft, summarise, sort and check. A person reads and approves anything that leaves the company. [Name] approves customer and supplier messages. [Name] approves anything involving money, contracts, investors or hiring. If the approver is unavailable, the item waits.
>
> **6. Ownership.** Company data and the work you produce for [Company] stay with [Company]. [Name] owns each approved tool and its access list.
>
> **7. When something goes wrong.** If sensitive data went into the wrong tool, or AI output went out unchecked, tell [Name] the same day. No blame for good-faith reports.
>
> **8. Review.** [Name] reviews this policy every [quarter] and after any incident.

## Roll it out in a week

- **Day 1.** Ask each team lead which AI tools their people use. Include personal accounts.
- **Day 2.** Pick the approved tools. Check each vendor's data page. Choose an owner for each.
- **Day 3.** Fill in the template. One person writes it. Two people read it.
- **Day 4.** Walk the team through it in 20 minutes. Show one example that is fine and one that is not.
- **Day 5.** Turn on the controls you can: business accounts, sign-in through your company login and one place to report problems.

Put a review date in the calendar. A policy without one is out of date in six months.
