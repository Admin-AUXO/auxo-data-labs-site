**In short.** The current Claude models can read a great deal at once, but reading more is not the same as answering well. The right documents and agreed definitions decide the answer. A bigger context window does not.

## How much Claude can read

Every current Claude model, from Haiku 5.5 to Fable 5.2, has a context window of one million tokens. Anthropic puts that at roughly 555,000 words. That is several long contracts, a year of board packs, or a large slice of a policy library in a single request.

That is useful. You can ask questions across a whole contract set, compare versions of a policy, or check a long report for internal consistency. Tasks that once needed careful slicing now fit in one go.

But there are three catches.

## Reading more costs more

You pay for every token the model reads, every time. Sending a full document library with each question is slow and expensive, especially at volume. Anthropic's pricing notes that very long requests can be billed at a different rate. Check the current pricing page before you design around it.

Prompt caching helps when the same large document is used again and again. A cached read costs a small fraction of a normal one. It only helps if the same content repeats. The [cost control post](/blog/cost-control-routing-caching-batching/) in this series covers it.

## The model needs the right documents

For most business questions, a small set of the right documents beats a large pile of mixed ones. This is what retrieval is for. A search step finds the few passages that matter, and only those go to the model.

Good retrieval depends on ordinary things done well:

- **One current version.** If your shared drive holds four copies of the pricing policy, the model may quote the wrong one with confidence.
- **Clear ownership.** Someone has to say which document is the source of truth.
- **Useful labels.** Dates, owners, departments and status make search far more accurate.
- **Access rules.** The model should only see what the person asking is allowed to see. That has to be enforced outside the model.

## The numbers have to agree

Documents are one thing. Data is another.

Ask a model, "What was our ARR last quarter?" If your CRM, billing system and finance sheet each define ARR differently, the model will pick one. It may not tell you which. It will still sound sure.

No context window fixes that. The fix is agreeing what each number means, where it comes from and who owns it. Then checking that the systems match.

## By function

**Finance.** Reading every contract in the data room to list renewal dates and change-of-control clauses works well with a large context. Answering "what is our runway?" depends on clean, reconciled data, not on reading more.

**Sales.** Summarising a long account history before a renewal is a good fit. Forecasting depends on a pipeline where stages mean the same thing to every rep.

**Support.** Reading a customer's full ticket history before a reply is a good fit. Reporting on churn risk depends on tickets that are tagged the same way every time.

**Engineering.** Checking a long design doc against your security policy works well. Answering "what changed in this release?" depends on release notes that are current and in one place.

## Try this

Pick one question your leadership team asks every month, such as "what is our net revenue retention?" Write down:

1. Every system or document that holds part of the answer.
2. Whether those sources agree on the definition of the key number.
3. Who would settle a disagreement.

Then ask Claude the question with only the documents you would trust. Ask again with everything in the folder. Compare the answers. The difference shows how much your data, not your model, is shaping the result.
