**In short:** a data silo is data held in one tool that other tools cannot see, so the same number differs between them. Fix it by agreeing what each number means, then connecting the core tools into one clean source in your own accounts.

Say you open the board deck, the finance model and the accounts side by side. The deck puts ARR at one number. The model says another. The accounts say a third.

Nobody made an error. Each tool is doing what it was set up to do. That is what data silos look like from the inside, and it is what an investor finds in diligence.

## What is a data silo?

A data silo is data held in one tool or team that others cannot easily see or reuse. Each silo has its own definitions.

<Callout>
A single source of truth does not mean one giant system.
</Callout>

It means each important number has one agreed definition, one owning system and one clean copy that everything else reads from.

## How can you tell you have one?

You rarely see a silo directly. You see its symptoms.

- **Numbers differ by tool.** Sales counts revenue when a deal is signed. Finance counts it when an invoice is issued. Both are right by their own rule.
- **Someone rebuilds the same report each month,** from three exports and a spreadsheet.
- **Nobody can say where a number came from.**
- **Customers appear more than once.** The same company is "Acme", "Acme Ltd" and "ACME Limited" across the CRM, billing and support.
- **Every meeting starts with a debate about whose figure is right.**

These problems are common. In MuleSoft's 2026 survey, the average organisation ran 957 applications and only 27% of them were connected ([MuleSoft](https://blogs.mulesoft.com/agentic-perspectives/connectivity-benchmark-report/)). In its 2025 survey, 90% of organisations said data silos caused business obstacles ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/mulesoft-connectivity-benchmark-2025/)). Both are vendor surveys, so read them as a signal, not a measurement of your business.

## Why does AI make it worse?

People cope with silos. They know the finance number goes in the investor update and the sales number in the pipeline review.

An AI tool does not know that. Ask it for revenue and it picks a source, and you may never see which. Untidy data in gives untidy answers out, at speed, in good prose. That is more dangerous than a wrong spreadsheet, because it sounds right. See [Agree what the numbers mean before AI reads them](/blog/agree-what-the-numbers-mean-before-ai-reads-them/).

## The fix, in order

1. **Agree what each number means.** Start with the 10 to 20 figures that run the business: ARR, burn, runway, churn, pipeline. Write one sentence for each, including what is left out. Start from the decisions they serve: [Start with the decision, not the dashboard](/blog/start-with-the-decision-not-the-dashboard/).
2. **Pick the system that owns each number.** Revenue is owned by billing, not by the CRM. Headcount is owned by payroll. One owner per number ends most arguments.
3. **Connect the few core tools into one clean source.** Usually the CRM, billing, accounting and one or two operational tools, pulled into a single store in your own cloud accounts. Match records, such as the three versions of Acme, into one.
4. **Add data checks.** Rules that run every day: totals that must match between billing and accounts, fields that must not be empty, duplicates that get flagged. A failed check should tell a person.
5. **Name an owner.** One person is accountable for the definitions and the checks.

You own all of it. The data sits in your cloud accounts, under your logins. A foundation you cannot inspect is another silo.

## What to leave alone

Do not start with a big warehouse project. Without agreed definitions, it only collects your disagreements in one place.

Do not connect every tool either. Most of the value sits in a handful. Add more once the first set is trusted.

## How long does a first pass take?

It depends on how many tools are involved and how messy the history is. With the scope held to the core tools and key numbers, the first pass is measured in weeks.

Agreeing definitions is usually the slowest part, because it needs the people who own the numbers in one room. The technical connection is often quicker than that conversation.
